# How the fixer works

The fixer is a Python controller around Claude Sonnet 4.6 with local investigation and repair tools. It runs original public tests and simulator seeds, discovers healthy public scenario/profile controls, and retrieves bounded current contracts. Optional Jev decisions filter weak document matches while preserving authoritative evidence and uncertain results.

Sonnet records source-bound hypotheses, examines causal trace windows, composes immutable fault probes, and edits a staged source tree. Measured replay costs select additional coverage within the deadline. Public seeds, explicit probes, earlier passing experiments and every previously repaired obligation remain mandatory, even when another invariant still fails on that input. Acceptance requires public tests, reproduced progress, preserved obligations and deterministic repeats. A separate native Sonnet decision reviews the exact source hash; partial fixes require review.

Accepted patches are installed, checked again, and rolled back on verification failure. Source hashes and Python compilation reject stale or malformed edits. Spend reservations and bounded subprocesses protect final verification. Compaction preserves source-bound findings and the latest complete tool exchange. Cached replays count as information; actual new executions establish progress. Six information turns can trigger validation or an immutable diagnostic on both snapshots after rejection, or accepted source before a patch. Diagnostics do not promote source. Cancellation stops incomplete model output and local workers.

## Results

All 492 combined checks pass, with independent review complete. Two chained fixtures prove 0 → 1 → 2 repairs across 20 seeds per bug, deterministic traces and healthy controls using an offline review stub. The final frozen image separately solved all four lab bugs autonomously for $0.711097; both paid cases used one combined patch, so they do not demonstrate iterative discovery. A real Jev call passed in 0.497 seconds for $0.000026544.

The exact final `24e51ae` Linux image fixed **6/12 official practice bugs for $25.4619**, with every public-test and regression gate passing and no infrastructure errors or timeouts. Earlier images reached 7/12 after a recorded B1 reporting correction, then 6/12, 4/12 and 7/12. These trials exposed evidence, memory, control-coverage and cached-replay defects alongside incomplete model diagnosis and mismatched experiment comparisons. They do not establish an improvement in score or spend.
